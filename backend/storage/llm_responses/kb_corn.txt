SECTION: CORN / MAIZE (Zea mays) — COMPLETE CULTIVATION GUIDE

## OVERVIEW
Corn is a high-yield cereal crop widely grown in Indonesia for food, animal feed,
and industrial starch. It is more drought-tolerant than rice but very sensitive to
waterlogging. Corn has a critical moisture window during tasseling and silking.

---

## OPTIMAL GROWING CONDITIONS

Soil moisture: 50–75%
Soil pH: 5.8–7.0 (optimal 6.0–6.8)
Air temperature: 21–32°C (optimal 24–30°C)
Air humidity: 60–80%
Sunlight: minimum 8 hours per day (highly light-demanding)
Rainfall: 100–200 mm per month
Growing period: 90–120 days depending on variety

---

## GROWTH STAGES & WATER MANAGEMENT

Germination (0–7 days):
- Soil moisture: 60–70%
- Critical — drought at this stage causes uneven emergence
- Avoid waterlogging (seed rot risk)

Seedling / Early vegetative (7–30 days):
- Soil moisture: 55–70%
- Irrigate every 3–4 days
- Most drought-tolerant stage of corn

Rapid vegetative growth — V6 to V12 (30–55 days):
- Soil moisture: 60–75%
- Corn grows 5–8 cm per day — high water demand
- Nitrogen uptake peaks at this stage

Tasseling & Silking — MOST CRITICAL STAGE (55–75 days):
- Soil moisture: MUST stay above 55% at all times
- Drought of even 1–2 days at silking causes severe yield loss (30–50%)
- If soil_moisture < 40% during this stage: emergency irrigation required
- High temperature (>35°C) + low humidity (<50%) damages pollen viability

Grain filling (75–100 days):
- Soil moisture: 55–70%
- Gradual reduction acceptable in final 2 weeks
- Adequate potassium essential for starch accumulation

Maturation (100–120 days):
- Reduce irrigation — soil moisture: 40–50%
- Harvest when husk is dry and grain has black layer (physiological maturity)

---

## DISEASE MANAGEMENT FOR CORN

Northern Corn Leaf Blight (Exserohilum turcicum):
- Trigger: high humidity (>80%), temperature 18–27°C
- Symptoms: long cigar-shaped gray-green lesions on leaves
- Treatment: Mancozeb or Chlorothalonil spray
- Use resistant hybrid varieties

Ear Rot / Diplodia Stalk Rot (Stenocarpella maydis):
- Trigger: soil_moisture > 80%, high temperature after silking
- Symptoms: stalk rots from inside, white mold on ear
- Prevention: maintain soil drainage, avoid plant stress

Downy Mildew (Peronosclerospora maydis):
- Trigger: rainfall > 20 mm + humidity > 85% during seedling stage
- Symptoms: yellow striping on young leaves, white downy growth underneath
- Treatment: systemic fungicide (Metalaxyl) as seed treatment

Corn Smut (Ustilago maydis):
- Triggered by plant wounds during high humidity
- Symptoms: galls filled with black powdery spores
- Remove and burn galls before they rupture

## FERTILIZATION SCHEDULE

Basal application (at planting):
- Organic compost: 2 tons/hectare
- SP36 (phosphate): 100–150 kg/hectare
- KCl (potassium): 75 kg/hectare

First top-dressing (25–30 days after planting — V4 stage):
- Urea: 150 kg/hectare
- NPK 15-15-15: 100 kg/hectare

Second top-dressing (45–50 days after planting — V8 stage):
- Urea: 100 kg/hectare
- KCl: 50 kg/hectare

Fertilization rules:
- Apply Urea by placing in furrow beside plant, not on top (reduces volatilization)
- Do not fertilize when soil_moisture < 30% (poor uptake)
- Do not fertilize when rainfall_mm > 15 (leaching)

---

## IRRIGATION DECISION GUIDE FOR CORN

Sensor reading → Action:
- soil_moisture < 30%: Irrigate immediately — especially critical at tasseling
- soil_moisture 30–55%: Irrigate within 24 hours
- soil_moisture 55–75%: Normal condition
- soil_moisture > 80%: Stop irrigation, improve drainage — stalk rot risk

Critical alert for tasseling stage (day 55–75):
- If soil_moisture < 40% AND temperature > 32°C: emergency irrigation needed
- Yield loss is irreversible if silks dry out before pollination is complete

---

## HARVEST INDICATORS
- Husk turns completely brown and dry
- Black layer visible at kernel base (kernel milk line gone)
- Grain moisture: 30–35% at harvest; dry to 14% for storage
- Kernels are hard and dented (for field corn varieties)

---

## COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID
1. Insufficient irrigation during silking stage (most common yield loss cause)
2. Over-irrigating after pollination (stalk rot risk)
3. Too high plant density (reduces ear development, increases disease)
4. Skipping potassium fertilizer (weak stalks, lodging risk)
5. Harvesting too early before physiological maturity (poor grain quality)
