## OVERVIEW
Melon and watermelon belong to the Cucurbitaceae family and are grown in Indonesia
during the dry season (May–September) for best fruit quality. Both crops are highly
profitable but require strict water management — excess water dramatically reduces
fruit sweetness (Brix) and increases fungal disease.

---

## OPTIMAL GROWING CONDITIONS

Soil moisture: 50–70%
Soil pH: 6.0–7.0 (optimal 6.2–6.8)
Air temperature: 25–35°C (optimal 28–32°C)
Air humidity: 50–70% (above 80% increases powdery mildew and gummy stem blight)
Sunlight: minimum 8–10 hours per day (critical for sugar accumulation)
Rainfall: < 100 mm per month (dry season crop — rain damages fruit quality)
Growing period:
  - Melon: 60–75 days after transplanting
  - Watermelon: 65–80 days after transplanting

---

## GROWTH STAGES & WATER MANAGEMENT

Seedling (0–14 days in polybag nursery):
- Soil moisture: 60–70%
- Transplant at 2–3 true leaf stage
- Temperature: minimum 25°C

Early vegetative (day 1–20 after transplanting):
- Soil moisture: 60–70%
- Irrigate every 2 days via drip or furrow
- Train vines along trellis (melon) or along ground (watermelon)

Vine development (20–35 days):
- Soil moisture: 60–70%
- Begin side branch pinching: keep 1–2 main vines for melon
- For melon: manual pollination or bee access is critical

Flowering & Fruit Set — CRITICAL (35–50 days):
- Soil moisture: 55–65%
- Reduce irrigation slightly during pollination to improve fruit set
- Excess water causes flower drop and poor fruit set
- If rainfall_mm > 10 during this period: suspend irrigation completely

Fruit Development (50–65 days):
- Soil moisture: 55–70%
- Consistent irrigation for uniform fruit development
- Do NOT irrigate with overhead — wets fruit, causes disease

Sugar Accumulation / Ripening — MOST CRITICAL (65–75 days for melon):
- DRASTICALLY reduce irrigation: soil moisture: 40–50%
- Reducing water in final 2 weeks increases Brix (sweetness) by 2–4 degrees
- Any rainfall > 10 mm during this period reduces fruit sweetness
- For watermelon: final 10 days — soil moisture 35–45%

---

## DISEASE MANAGEMENT FOR MELON & WATERMELON

Powdery Mildew (Podosphaera xanthii):
- Trigger: humidity 50–70%, temperature 20–28°C, sudden temperature changes
- Symptoms: white powdery coating on upper leaf surface
- Treatment: sulfur-based fungicide or myclobutanil every 7 days
- Very common in dry season — preventive spraying recommended

Downy Mildew (Pseudoperonospora cubensis):
- Trigger: humidity > 85%, temperature 15–25°C, morning dew
- Symptoms: yellow angular lesions on upper leaf, gray mold underneath
- Treatment: Metalaxyl + Mancozeb spray immediately
- Spreads rapidly in cool humid weather

Fusarium Wilt (Fusarium oxysporum f.sp. melonis / niveum):
- Trigger: soil_pH > 7.5 or < 5.5, soil_moisture < 25%, high temperature
- Symptoms: sudden wilting, brown vascular tissue, often kills plant
- Prevention: Trichoderma soil amendment, pH management (6.0–7.0)
- Use grafted seedlings on resistant rootstock for high-risk fields

Gummy Stem Blight (Didymella bryoniae):
- Trigger: humidity > 85%, plant wounds, overhead irrigation
- Symptoms: tan lesions with dark gummy exudate on stems
- Treatment: Iprodione or Thiophanate-methyl
- Avoid any stem wounding during training

Anthracnose (Colletotrichum orbiculare):
- Trigger: rainfall > 15 mm, warm temperatures
- Symptoms: dark sunken lesions on fruits, rapid fruit rot
- Treatment: Mancozeb or Azoxystrobin spray
- Critical to control — ruins market value of fruit

## FERTILIZATION SCHEDULE

Basal (before transplanting):
- Organic compost: 15 tons/hectare
- SP36: 200 kg/hectare
- KCl: 100 kg/hectare

Vegetative top-dressing (every 10 days — days 10 to 35):
- NPK 20-10-10: high nitrogen for rapid vine growth
- 3–5 grams per plant per application

Fruit development top-dressing (days 35–65):
- NPK 10-10-20 or KNO3: shift to high potassium
- Calcium nitrate: 3 g/plant every 10 days (prevents blossom-end rot)

Sugar accumulation phase (days 55–75):
- Stop nitrogen fertilizer completely
- Potassium sulfate foliar spray (K2SO4) to boost Brix
- Calcium foliar spray for skin firmness

---

## IRRIGATION DECISION GUIDE FOR MELON & WATERMELON

Sensor reading → Action:
- soil_moisture < 35%: Irrigate — especially during vine/fruit development
- soil_moisture 35–55%: Normal for ripening phase
- soil_moisture 55–70%: Normal for vegetative/fruit development phase
- soil_moisture > 75%: Stop irrigation — powdery mildew and gummy stem blight risk

Sweetness management rule:
- Final 2 weeks before harvest: target soil_moisture 35–50% deliberately
- Any irrigation above 55% in final 2 weeks reduces Brix significantly

---

## COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID
1. Planting after cucumber or pumpkin (same Cucurbitaceae disease pool)
2. Overhead irrigation (destroys fruit appearance and spreads disease)
3. Over-irrigating during ripening (kills sweetness — biggest quality issue)
4. Not pinching lateral shoots in melon (too many fruits, all small)
5. Harvesting too early — check slip (melon separates from vine) or tapping
6. Insufficient sunlight — at least 8 hours daily required for high Brix
