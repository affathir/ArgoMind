# ArgoMind Knowledge Base — Chili (Cabai) Cultivation Guide
# Language: English

==============================================================================
SECTION: CHILI PEPPER (Capsicum annuum / Capsicum frutescens) — CULTIVATION GUIDE
==============================================================================

## OVERVIEW
Chili is one of the highest-value vegetable crops in Indonesia. It is highly
sensitive to waterlogging, fusarium wilt, and temperature extremes. Proper
drainage and pH management are the most critical success factors.

---

## OPTIMAL GROWING CONDITIONS

Soil moisture: 50–70%
Soil pH: 6.0–7.0 (strict — fusarium wilt risk above 7.5)
Air temperature: 24–32°C (optimal 26–30°C)
Air humidity: 60–80%
Sunlight: minimum 8 hours per day
Rainfall: 100–200 mm per month
Growing period: 90–120 days from transplanting to first harvest
Continuous harvest: every 3–5 days for up to 6 months

---

## GROWTH STAGES & WATER MANAGEMENT

Seedling stage (0–30 days in nursery):
- Soil moisture: 60–70%
- Avoid overwatering — root rot risk is high at this stage
- Temperature: minimum 22°C for good germination

Transplanting stage (day 1–14 after transplanting):
- Water daily but lightly — do not flood
- Soil moisture: 55–65%

Vegetative stage (14–45 days after transplanting):
- Soil moisture: 55–70%
- Irrigate every 2–3 days depending on weather
- Avoid wetting leaves during irrigation (use drip or furrow)

Flowering stage (45–70 days):
- CRITICAL — drought at this stage causes flower drop
- Soil moisture must stay above 50% at all times
- If soil moisture < 35%: irrigate immediately to prevent flower abortion

Fruiting stage (70–120 days):
- Soil moisture: 55–65%
- Reduce irrigation slightly 2 weeks before harvest for better fruit color
- Rainfall > 30 mm/day increases anthracnose risk significantly

---

## DISEASE MANAGEMENT FOR CHILI

Fusarium Wilt (Fusarium oxysporum f.sp. capsici):
- Trigger conditions: soil_ph > 7.5, temperature > 32°C, soil_moisture < 25%
- Symptoms: sudden wilting from one side, brown vascular tissue
- Treatment: remove infected plant, drench Carbendazim to planting hole
- Prevention: Trichoderma harzianum soil amendment before planting

Anthracnose / Fruit Rot (Colletotrichum spp.):
- Trigger conditions: rainfall > 20 mm/day, humidity > 85%
- Symptoms: sunken dark lesions on fruits, orange spore masses
- Treatment: Mancozeb or Propineb spray every 7 days during rainy season
- Prevention: avoid wetting fruits during irrigation

Bacterial Wilt (Ralstonia solanacearum):
- Trigger conditions: high temperature (32–38°C), high moisture
- Symptoms: rapid wilting, slimy ooze from stem cross-section
- No chemical cure — remove and burn infected plants immediately

Leaf Curl Virus (Gemini virus, transmitted by whiteflies):
- Symptoms: upward leaf curling, yellowing, stunted growth
- Control: manage whitefly with imidacloprid or yellow sticky traps

Powdery Mildew:
- Trigger conditions: humidity < 50%, temperature 20–28°C
- Symptoms: white powdery coating on leaves
- Treatment: sulfur-based fungicide or neem oil spray

---

## FERTILIZATION SCHEDULE

Basal (1 week before transplanting):
- Organic compost: 15–20 tons/hectare
- SP36: 150 kg/hectare
- KCl: 100 kg/hectare

Vegetative phase top-dressing (every 10 days):
- NPK 15-15-15: 5 grams per plant
- Urea: 3 grams per plant

Flowering & fruiting phase (every 7 days):
- Increase potassium: NPK 12-12-17 or KNO3
- Add calcium-boron foliar spray to prevent blossom-end rot

---

## IRRIGATION DECISION GUIDE FOR CHILI

Sensor reading → Action:
- soil_moisture < 30%: Irrigate immediately — flower drop risk
- soil_moisture 30–50%: Irrigate within 12 hours
- soil_moisture 50–70%: Normal condition
- soil_moisture > 75%: Stop irrigation, open drainage — anthracnose risk

Rainfall guidance:
- rainfall_mm > 20: Delay all pesticide/fungicide application
- rainfall_mm > 50: Check drainage, inspect for anthracnose after rain

---

## COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID
1. Planting chili after tomato or eggplant (same Solanaceae family — wilt risk)
2. Overhead irrigation during flowering (causes flower drop + disease)
3. Ignoring pH above 7.5 (Fusarium wilt risk multiplies)
4. Excessive nitrogen during fruiting (reduces fruit quality, increases disease)
5. Not rotating crops — continuous chili planting builds up soil pathogens
