# Language: English

SECTION: LONG BEANS (Vigna unguiculata subsp. sesquipedalis) &
         SOYBEAN (Glycine max) — COMPLETE CULTIVATION GUIDE
==============================================================================

## OVERVIEW
Long beans and soybean are leguminous crops that fix atmospheric nitrogen through
root nodule bacteria (Rhizobium). This makes them excellent rotation crops after
rice or corn. They improve soil fertility for the next planting season.
Both crops are moderately drought-tolerant but sensitive to waterlogging.

---

## OPTIMAL GROWING CONDITIONS — LONG BEANS

Soil moisture: 50–70%
Soil pH: 6.0–7.0 (optimal 6.2–6.8)
Air temperature: 22–35°C (optimal 25–30°C)
Air humidity: 60–80%
Sunlight: minimum 8 hours per day
Growing period: 45–60 days from planting to first harvest
Continuous harvest: every 3–5 days for 2–3 months

## OPTIMAL GROWING CONDITIONS — SOYBEAN

Soil moisture: 50–70% (drought-tolerant after establishment)
Soil pH: 6.0–7.0 (Rhizobium bacteria prefer near-neutral pH)
Air temperature: 22–32°C (optimal 25–28°C)
Air humidity: 60–80%
Sunlight: photoperiod-sensitive — performs best with 12–14 hours daylight
Growing period: 75–100 days depending on variety

---

## GROWTH STAGES & WATER MANAGEMENT — SOYBEAN

Germination & Emergence (0–10 days):
- Soil moisture: 60–70%
- Critical — drought causes patchy emergence; waterlogging causes damping-off
- Temperature: minimum 18°C for germination

Vegetative growth (10–35 days):
- Soil moisture: 55–70%
- Most drought-tolerant stage
- Inoculate with Bradyrhizobium japonicum if not previously grown in field
- Nodule formation begins — do NOT apply nitrogen fertilizer (inhibits nodulation)

Flowering — CRITICAL STAGE (35–55 days):
- Soil moisture: 60–75%
- Drought at flowering causes pod abortion (50–70% yield reduction)
- If soil_moisture < 40%: irrigate immediately
- Do not waterlog — increases purple seed stain and stem rot

Pod filling — MOST CRITICAL (55–80 days):
- Soil moisture: 65–75%
- Pod filling requires consistent moisture — any drought directly reduces seed size
- Temperature > 35°C + drought dramatically reduces protein content

Maturation (80–100 days):
- Reduce irrigation — soil moisture: 40–50%
- Harvest when 90% of pods have turned brown and rattle when shaken

---

## GROWTH STAGES & WATER MANAGEMENT — LONG BEANS

Germination (0–7 days):
- Soil moisture: 60–70%; avoid standing water

Vegetative (7–25 days):
- Soil moisture: 55–70%
- Install trellis/bamboo support at 20 cm plant height

Flowering & Harvest (25–60 days):
- Soil moisture: 60–70%
- Harvest pods at 35–40 cm length (before seeds bulge)
- Reduce irrigation slightly 3 days before harvest for better pod texture

---

## DISEASE MANAGEMENT — LONG BEANS & SOYBEAN

Cercospora Leaf Spot (Cercospora canescens / kikuchii):
- Trigger: humidity > 80%, temperature 25–30°C
- Symptoms: reddish-brown circular spots on leaves; purple stain on soybean seeds
- Treatment: Mancozeb or Thiophanate-methyl spray every 10 days

Soybean Rust (Phakopsora pachyrhizi):
- Trigger: humidity > 75%, temperature 15–28°C, extended wet periods
- Symptoms: small tan lesions with orange pustules on undersides of leaves
- Most destructive soybean disease in Indonesia
- Treatment: Trifloxystrobin or Azoxystrobin — apply at first symptom

Root Rot (Phytophthora sojae / Pythium spp.):
- Trigger: soil_moisture > 80%, pH < 5.5, poor drainage
- Symptoms: damping-off in seedlings, brown rotting taproot
- Prevention: improve drainage, pH management, avoid waterlogging

Mosaic Virus (Soybean Mosaic Virus — transmitted by aphids):
- Symptoms: mottled yellowing, leaf distortion, reduced pod set
- Control: manage aphid populations with imidacloprid or neem oil
- Use virus-free certified seeds

Bacterial Pustule (Xanthomonas axonopodis):
- Trigger: high humidity + rain splash + mechanical wounds
- Symptoms: small yellow-green spots with raised centers on leaves
- Treatment: copper-based bactericide

## NITROGEN FIXATION — KEY BENEFIT OF LEGUMES

Both crops fix atmospheric nitrogen:
- Long beans: 50–100 kg N/hectare per season
- Soybean: 100–200 kg N/hectare per season

To maximize nitrogen fixation:
- Inoculate seeds with Rhizobium/Bradyrhizobium before planting
- Soil pH must be 6.0–7.0 for bacteria to thrive
- Do NOT apply nitrogen fertilizer (Urea/NPK) after establishment
- Leave root system in soil after harvest to release nitrogen for next crop

---

## FERTILIZATION SCHEDULE — SOYBEAN

Basal (at planting):
- Organic compost: 2 tons/hectare
- SP36: 100 kg/hectare
- KCl: 75 kg/hectare
- Dolomite: 500 kg/hectare if pH below 6.0

Starter nitrogen (at planting only):
- Urea: 25–50 kg/hectare (small amount to support early growth before nodulation)

After nodulation (DO NOT apply nitrogen):
- Potassium sulfate: 50 kg/hectare at pod filling stage
- Foliar spray: boron + molybdenum (improves nodulation efficiency)

---

## IRRIGATION DECISION GUIDE

Sensor reading → Action:
- soil_moisture < 35%: Irrigate immediately — critical if at flowering or pod filling
- soil_moisture 35–50%: Irrigate within 24 hours
- soil_moisture 50–70%: Normal condition
- soil_moisture > 80%: Stop irrigation — root rot and bacterial disease risk

Critical alert for soybean:
- If soil_moisture < 40% AND at flowering stage (day 35–55): emergency irrigation
- Yield loss from drought at flowering is permanent and not recoverable

---

## ROTATION BENEFIT FOR NEXT SEASON
After harvest of legumes:
- Reduce nitrogen fertilizer by 25–30% for next corn or rice crop
- Soil organic matter increases from root residues
- Naturally suppresses some soil-borne pathogens through microbial diversity
