# Language: English

SECTION: TOMATO (Solanum lycopersicum) — COMPLETE CULTIVATION GUIDE

## OVERVIEW
Tomato is a high-value vegetable crop in Indonesia, grown year-round in highlands
and during dry season in lowlands. It belongs to the Solanaceae family and shares
disease risks with chili, eggplant, and potato. Consistent moisture and strict pH
management are the most important factors for disease prevention.

---

## OPTIMAL GROWING CONDITIONS

Soil moisture: 55–75%
Soil pH: 6.0–6.8 (strict — below 5.5 causes calcium deficiency; above 7.0 increases Fusarium risk)
Air temperature: 20–28°C (optimal 22–26°C; above 32°C reduces fruit set significantly)
Air humidity: 60–80% (above 85% triggers early blight and late blight)
Sunlight: minimum 8 hours per day
Rainfall: 100–150 mm per month (excess rain dramatically increases disease)
Growing period: 60–90 days from transplanting to first harvest
Continuous harvest: every 2–3 days for up to 4 months

---

## GROWTH STAGES & WATER MANAGEMENT

Seedling / Nursery (0–25 days):
- Soil moisture: 60–70%
- Water lightly twice daily; avoid waterlogging
- Temperature: 22–28°C for good germination

Transplanting / Establishment (day 1–14 after transplanting):
- Water daily; soil moisture 60–70%
- Avoid wetting the crown and stem (damping-off risk)

Vegetative stage (14–35 days):
- Soil moisture: 60–70%
- Irrigate every 2 days
- Begin staking when plant reaches 30 cm height

Flowering stage (35–55 days):
- CRITICAL for fruit set
- Soil moisture: 65–75%
- Drought causes flower drop; excess moisture causes fruit cracking later
- Temperature > 32°C at night causes blossom drop — shade if necessary

Fruit development (55–90 days):
- Soil moisture: 60–70% — keep consistent (fluctuations cause blossom-end rot)
- Avoid any moisture stress — causes fruit cracking when re-irrigated
- Calcium foliar spray every 2 weeks for blossom-end rot prevention

---

## DISEASE MANAGEMENT FOR TOMATO

Early Blight (Alternaria solani):
- Trigger: humidity > 80%, temperature 24–29°C, aging lower leaves
- Symptoms: dark brown concentric ring lesions on older leaves
- Treatment: Mancozeb or Chlorothalonil spray every 7 days
- Remove infected lower leaves and destroy

Late Blight (Phytophthora infestans):
- Trigger: cool temperatures (15–22°C) + high humidity (>90%) + rainfall
- Symptoms: water-soaked lesions on leaves, white mold beneath, rapid spread
- Treatment: Metalaxyl + Mancozeb (systemic + contact) immediately
- Most destructive tomato disease — act within 24 hours of first symptoms

Fusarium Crown and Root Rot (Fusarium oxysporum f.sp. lycopersici):
- Trigger: soil_ph > 7.0, soil_moisture < 30%, high temperature
- Symptoms: yellowing one side, brown vascular tissue
- Prevention: Trichoderma soil amendment, pH management
- No cure after infection — remove plant immediately

Bacterial Wilt (Ralstonia solanacearum):
- Trigger: high temperature + high moisture
- Symptoms: rapid wilting, milky stream when stem submerged in water
- No chemical control — remove and destroy entire plant and surrounding soil

Blossom-End Rot (calcium deficiency — not a disease):
- Trigger: irregular irrigation, calcium deficiency, soil_moisture fluctuation
- Symptoms: dark sunken area at blossom end of fruit
- Treatment: calcium nitrate foliar spray (Ca(NO3)2) every 10 days
- Fix irrigation schedule to prevent moisture fluctuation

---

## FERTILIZATION SCHEDULE

Basal (1 week before transplanting):
- Organic compost: 20–25 tons/hectare
- SP36: 200 kg/hectare
- KCl: 100 kg/hectare

Vegetative top-dressing (every 10 days):
- NPK 15-15-15: 5–7 grams per plant

Flowering & fruiting (every 7–10 days):
- High potassium formulation: NPK 12-12-17 or KNO3
- Calcium-boron foliar spray every 14 days (blossom-end rot prevention)
- Magnesium sulfate (MgSO4) foliar at 2 g/liter if leaf interveinal yellowing appears

---

## IRRIGATION DECISION GUIDE FOR TOMATO

Sensor reading → Action:
- soil_moisture < 40%: Irrigate immediately — blossom drop and wilt risk
- soil_moisture 40–55%: Irrigate within 12 hours
- soil_moisture 55–75%: Normal condition
- soil_moisture > 80%: Stop irrigation — late blight and root rot risk

Rainfall guidance:
- rainfall_mm > 20: Spray preventive fungicide within 24 hours (late blight risk)
- After heavy rain: check for standing water around stem base

---

## COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID
1. Planting after chili, eggplant, or potato (same Solanaceae family)
2. Irregular irrigation causing soil moisture fluctuation (blossom-end rot)
3. Overhead irrigation (water on leaves increases disease dramatically)
4. Ignoring early blight on lower leaves (spreads rapidly to whole plant)
5. Not removing suckers (axillary shoots) — reduces yield and increases disease
6. Harvesting too late — overripe fruits attract pests and spread disease
