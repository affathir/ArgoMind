SECTION: IRRIGATION MANAGEMENT
==============================================================================

## Irrigation Decision Guide by Soil Moisture Level

CRITICAL — soil moisture < 20%:
- Irrigate IMMEDIATELY — all crop types
- Duration: 45–60 minutes for drip irrigation; 30 minutes for sprinkler
- Frequency: daily until moisture reaches 50%
- Warning: do NOT irrigate during peak heat (10:00–15:00) — risk of leaf burn
- Best time: before 09:00 or after 16:00

LOW — soil moisture 20–35%:
- Irrigate within 6–12 hours
- Duration: 30–45 minutes
- Monitor every 6 hours
- Check weather forecast — postpone if rain > 5 mm predicted within 12 hours

NORMAL — soil moisture 35–65%:
- Regular scheduled irrigation
- Adjust based on weather: skip if rain > 5 mm expected
- Morning irrigation preferred (reduces fungal disease risk)

HIGH — soil moisture 65–80%:
- No irrigation needed
- Monitor drainage channels

CRITICAL HIGH — soil moisture > 80%:
- STOP all irrigation immediately
- Open drainage channels and check for blockages
- Increased risk of root rot (Pythium, Fusarium), stem rot, and leaf diseases
- If waterlogging persists > 24 hours: check for root rot symptoms

## Irrigation Timing Guidelines

Best irrigation times:
- Early morning: 06:00–09:00 (water absorbs before evaporation peaks)
- Late afternoon: 16:00–18:00 (reduces fungal risk vs. night irrigation)

Irrigation methods ranked by efficiency:
1. Drip irrigation — most efficient, reduces leaf wetness and disease
2. Furrow irrigation — good for row crops (corn, soybean)
3. Sprinkler — avoid during flowering (flower drop) and fruiting (disease)
4. Flood irrigation — only for paddy rice

Mulching to conserve soil moisture:
- Straw mulch (5 cm thick): reduces evaporation by 40–60%
- Black plastic mulch: best for chili, tomato, melon
- Apply mulch after first irrigation, not before

## Raising pH (Soil Too Acidic)

pH below 5.0 (Very Acidic — act immediately):
- Apply 2–3 tons agricultural lime (CaCO3) per hectare
- Or dolomite (CaMg(CO3)2): 2 tons/hectare — also adds magnesium
- Mix into top 20 cm of soil by plowing
- Wait 4–6 weeks before planting after liming
- Re-test pH after 6 weeks

pH 5.0–5.5 (Moderately Acidic):
- Apply 1–1.5 tons dolomite per hectare
- Add organic compost (2–3 tons/hectare) for buffering effect
- Suitable for rice; needs correction for most vegetables

pH 5.5–6.0 (Slightly Acidic — acceptable for most crops):
- Maintain with regular compost applications
- Monitor annually

## Lowering pH (Soil Too Alkaline)

pH above 7.5 (Too Alkaline — Fusarium wilt risk high):
- Apply elemental sulfur: 200–500 kg/hectare per 1 unit pH reduction
- Process is slow: allow 6–8 weeks for full effect
- Add organic matter (peat, compost) to buffer and gradually acidify
- Apply iron sulfate (FeSO4) for faster action

pH 7.0–7.5 (Slightly Alkaline):
- Regular compost application gradually acidifies
- Avoid any lime application
- Use acidifying fertilizers: ammonium sulfate, ammonium phosphate

## pH Impact on Nutrient Availability

pH 4.0–5.5: Aluminum and manganese toxicity; phosphorus locked up
pH 5.5–6.5: OPTIMAL — all nutrients available
pH 6.5–7.5: Slightly reduced phosphorus availability; iron deficiency begins
pH above 7.5: Iron, zinc, manganese become unavailable; Fusarium risk high

## Fertilization Decision by Weather

When to DELAY or SKIP fertilization:
- Rainfall > 15 mm/day: leaching risk — all granular fertilizers
- Rainfall > 30 mm/day: delay by 3 days minimum
- Temperature > 35°C: urea volatilizes rapidly — delay or apply in furrow
- Soil moisture < 30%: poor nutrient uptake — irrigate first, then fertilize
- Soil moisture > 80%: waterlogging reduces root uptake

Best conditions for fertilization:
- Soil moisture: 50–70%
- Temperature: 20–32°C
- No rain predicted for next 24 hours
- Time of day: 06:00–09:00 (morning)

## Nitrogen (N) Management

Urea (46% N):
- Use for vegetative growth stages
- Do NOT use during fruiting/flowering — excess N causes flower drop
- Application: place 5 cm beside plant base, cover with soil (reduces volatilization)
- Split application every 2–3 weeks is more effective than single large dose

Ammonium sulfate (21% N):
- Preferred for alkaline soils (acidifying effect)
- Lower volatilization than urea
- Good choice when soil pH > 7.0

Calcium nitrate (15.5% N):
- Best for fruiting crops (tomato, chili, melon)
- Provides calcium simultaneously (prevents blossom-end rot)
- Water-soluble — can be applied via drip irrigation

## Phosphorus (P) Management

SP36 (36% P2O5):
- Apply as basal fertilizer before planting
- Mixes poorly with lime — do not apply together
- Activates root development and flowering

TSP (46% P2O5):
- Higher concentration, lower application rate needed
- Use in soil with pH 5.5–7.0 for best availability

## Potassium (K) Management

KCl (60% K2O):
- Standard potassium source
- Apply during fruiting for starch/sugar accumulation (corn, potato, melon)
- Do not apply to acid soils (pH < 5.5) — increases toxicity

KNO3 (potassium nitrate — 13% N, 44% K):
- Premium choice for fruiting phase
- Water-soluble — excellent for fertigation via drip

## Symptoms of Nutrient Deficiency

Nitrogen deficiency:
- Pale yellow leaves starting from oldest (bottom) leaves
- Thin stems, poor growth
- Action: apply urea 1 tablespoon per 10 liters water as drench

Phosphorus deficiency:
- Reddish-purple coloration on leaf undersides
- Stunted roots, delayed maturity
- Action: apply SP36 or foliar phosphate spray

Potassium deficiency:
- Brown scorching on leaf edges (tip and margins)
- Weak stems, lodging in corn
- Reduced fruit size and sweetness
- Action: apply KCl or potassium sulfate

Calcium deficiency:
- Blossom-end rot in tomato, chili, melon
- Tip burn in lettuce and leafy vegetables
- Action: calcium nitrate foliar spray (2 g/liter)

Magnesium deficiency:
- Interveinal yellowing (veins stay green, area between turns yellow)
- Starts from older leaves
- Action: magnesium sulfate (MgSO4) foliar 2 g/liter

## Drought Management (Rainfall < 2 mm for more than 7 days)

Immediate actions:
- Priority irrigation: crops at flowering or fruiting stage
- Apply straw mulch 5–7 cm thick to reduce evaporation
- Spray water on leaf canopy in early morning (foliar cooling)
- Reduce plant density if possible to lower competition
- Add silicon fertilizer to improve drought tolerance

Irrigation schedule during drought:
- High-priority crops: irrigate every day
- Medium-priority: every 2 days
- Before irrigation: check forecast — delay if rain likely within 24 hours

## Heavy Rainfall Management (> 50 mm/day)

Immediate actions:
- Ensure all drainage channels are open and not blocked
- Create temporary diversion ditches if waterlogging occurs
- DELAY all fertilizer application — leaching risk
- DELAY all pesticide/fungicide — runoff makes it ineffective
- After rain: inspect plants for fungal disease symptoms within 24–48 hours

Post-rain disease prevention:
- Spray preventive fungicide within 24 hours after heavy rain
- Priority for: tomato (late blight), potato (late blight), melon (downy mildew)
- Check soil surface for crusting — break with light cultivation if needed

## High Temperature Management (> 38°C)

- Irrigate in early morning AND late afternoon (two sessions)
- Install shade net 30–50% for temperature-sensitive crops
- Spray cool water on leaf canopy at midday
- Do NOT apply urea or granular fertilizer during extreme heat
- Monitor humidity closely — hot dry conditions trigger spider mites

## Crop Rotation & Planting Calendar

Recommended rotation to prevent disease buildup:
- Season 1: Rice or Corn (Gramineae)
- Season 2: Legumes — Soybean or Long Beans (fix nitrogen, break disease cycle)
- Season 3: Leafy Vegetables
- Season 4: Return to Rice or Corn

Never plant same family consecutively:
- Solanaceae: tomato, chili, eggplant, potato
- Cucurbitaceae: watermelon, melon, cucumber, pumpkin
- Gramineae: rice, corn, wheat

Optimal planting calendar for Indonesia:
- Rice (wet season): October–November
- Rice (dry season): March–April
- Corn: October–November or February–March
- Chili: May–June (lower disease pressure)
- Tomato: May–July (highland), year-round highland
- Soybean: October–December
- Melon/Watermelon: April–August (dry season)
- Potato: June–September (highland dry season)
