## OVERVIEW
Rice is the primary staple crop in Indonesia and Southeast Asia. It is cultivated
in flooded paddies (wet rice) or dryland conditions (upland rice). Optimal
management of water, soil pH, temperature, and disease is critical for high yield.

---

## OPTIMAL GROWING CONDITIONS

Soil moisture: 40–70% (flooded at 2–5 cm for wet rice during vegetative phase)
Soil pH: 5.5–7.0 (ideal: 6.0–6.5)
Air temperature: 22–32°C (optimal 25–28°C)
Air humidity: 70–85%
Sunlight: minimum 6 hours per day
Rainfall: 150–300 mm per month during growing season
Growing period: 100–145 days depending on variety

---

## GROWTH STAGES & WATER MANAGEMENT

Stage 1 — Seedling (0–14 days):
- Maintain soil moisture at 60–70%
- Do not flood at this stage
- Temperature must stay above 20°C for germination

Stage 2 — Vegetative / Tillering (15–60 days):
- Flood with 2–5 cm standing water
- This is the most critical irrigation period
- Soil moisture must not drop below 50%

Stage 3 — Reproductive / Panicle Initiation (60–80 days):
- Maintain 2–3 cm water depth
- Do not allow soil to dry — drought stress at this stage causes empty grains
- Monitor closely for blast disease

Stage 4 — Grain Filling (80–100 days):
- Gradually reduce water
- Maintain soil moisture at 40–60%

Stage 5 — Ripening (100–120 days):
- Drain the field 7–10 days before harvest
- Soil moisture: 30–40%

---

## DISEASE MANAGEMENT FOR RICE

Blast Disease (most critical for rice):
- Trigger sensor values: soil_moisture < 25%, humidity < 40%, temperature 28–35°C
- Action: Irrigate immediately + spray Tricyclazole fungicide
- Resistant varieties: Ciherang, Inpari 32, IR64

Sheath Blight (Rhizoctonia solani):
- Favored by: high humidity (>85%), high nitrogen, dense planting
- Symptoms: oval grayish-white lesions on leaf sheath
- Treatment: Hexaconazole or Validamycin fungicide

Bacterial Leaf Blight (Xanthomonas oryzae):
- Symptoms: water-soaked margins on leaves, yellowing from edges
- Prevention: avoid excessive nitrogen, use resistant varieties
- No effective chemical control — focus on prevention

Brown Plant Hopper (Nilaparvata lugens):
- Not detectable by sensors — visual inspection required
- High risk when plant density is too high

---

## FERTILIZATION SCHEDULE

Basal (before transplanting):
- Organic compost: 2–3 tons/hectare
- SP36 (phosphate): 100 kg/hectare
- KCl (potassium): 50 kg/hectare

First top-dressing (21–25 days after transplanting):
- Urea: 100 kg/hectare
- NPK: 150 kg/hectare

Second top-dressing (40–45 days after transplanting):
- Urea: 75 kg/hectare
- KCl: 50 kg/hectare

Fertilization rules:
- NEVER fertilize when rainfall > 15 mm/day (leaching risk)
- NEVER fertilize when temperature > 35°C (volatilization of urea)
- Best time: early morning (06:00–09:00) after dew

---

## IRRIGATION DECISION GUIDE FOR RICE

Sensor reading → Action:
- soil_moisture < 20%: CRITICAL — flood immediately to 3–5 cm
- soil_moisture 20–40%: Irrigate within 6 hours
- soil_moisture 40–70%: Normal — scheduled irrigation
- soil_moisture > 80%: Open drainage — risk of root rot and blast

---

## HARVEST INDICATORS
- 80–85% of grains have turned golden yellow
- Panicles bend downward due to grain weight
- Grain moisture content: 20–25% (best for milling at 14%)
- Days from heading: approximately 25–35 days

---

## COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID
1. Over-irrigating during ripening stage (causes lodging)
2. Applying urea during drought or extreme heat (>35°C)
3. Planting the same variety continuously (reduces blast resistance)
4. Ignoring pH below 5.5 (reduces nutrient availability significantly)
5. Burning rice straw in field (destroys beneficial microbes)
